I’m a little late to the game, but this string of words is the first in my attempt to keep a digital garden: I've come across a number of different variations but the general consensus seems to be somewhere between a thought dump, personal wiki, and experimental knowledge base that is growing and evolving continuously, kind of reminiscent of personal sites from the early 90s to 00s.

In the spirit of keeping my personal take true to form, weeds and all, expect to see writing in varying stages of polish—from brain farts to unfinished drafts to working notes. I might prune and cultivate as I go but, for the most part, I will be using this space to share my musings without the pressure of a more traditional, blog-like approach.

Structurally speaking, my digital garden is currently set up more like a chronological blog than something wild and free form. As more of my thoughts take root here, my goal is to eventually build an interconnected web of knowledge and insights that I've gleaned through work and play. At some point, I'd like to work towards incorporating [growth stages](https://freezing.cool/notes/maturity-identification), [bi-directional links](https://maggieappleton.com/bidirectionals), and maybe something closer to a network-like structure and navigation.

In the meantime, these are a few of the digital gardens that have inspired me to start my own and my approach:
- [Maggie Appleton's garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden)
- [Andy's working notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/)
- [Utsob Roy's hermitage](https://hermitage.utsob.me/)

If you're interested in digital gardening, I took down a few notes on the technicalities of [[using Obsidian as a CMS]], but my approach is pretty basic and barebones in its current state; better people than me have [collated dozens of resources](https://github.com/MaggieAppleton/digital-gardeners) to help others get started. Previously, I've used [TiddlyWiki](https://tiddlywiki.com/) for notes on game mechanics (lol) and appreciated the low barrier to entry.
